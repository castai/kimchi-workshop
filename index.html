03The rules

  1. Fix the app, not the tests. Everything under tests/ is read-only. If a test looks wrong, look again.
  2. The docstrings are the spec. Where code and docstrings disagree, the docstring describes intended behavior. Make the code honest.
  3. Small, verifiable changes. Re-run the suite after every edit. A broken intermediate step costs you time, not points.
  4. Green visible suite is the floor, not the ceiling. Your PR is also scored by a verification suite you cannot see — production will tell you about it in the PR comments.

04Submit — and understand the lifecycle

  1. Fork the repo, push your work, open a PR titled [submission] <your-handle> - <what you shipped>.
  2. Fill the PR template block exactly: 
    # in the PR body
attendee: <your-handle>
event: <event-code your facilitator announced>
cost_usd: <session spend from app.kimchi.dev>
review_score:
  3. Attach your Kimchi usage CSV export to the PR (from app.kimchi.dev/usage, filtered by attendee:<handle>). No CSV, no leaderboard — spend claims are verified.

Your PR never merges — by design. It is a scoring vessel, not a contribution to the repo. Scoring runs on it, you get ranked, production tells its story in your PR's comments, and then the PR is closed unmerged. That keeps the seed repo identical for every participant after you. Your score and your PR comments stay; the repo doesn't change.

How a score becomes a rank: CI scores your PR automatically (visible suite + hidden production scenario). A facilitator reviews and labels it accepted. Within a minute you appear on the Leaderboard tab up top — event dropdown, top-N, search, 60-second refresh.

05Scoring

output     = tests_passed × 10 + (review_score / 100) × 50
efficiency = ±20 based on your spend vs the budget
points     = output + efficiency

A cheap no-op always loses to real work. Production failures cost you more than any spend ever will.
